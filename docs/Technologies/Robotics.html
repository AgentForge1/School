🔥 Starter – Where Are Robots Used?

Robots have many uses today. Name at least three different areas where robots are used.

Discuss with your partner — be ready to share your ideas.

Show suggested answer
  • Industry – e.g. manufacturing of food and products
  • Transport – e.g. self-driving trains and cars
  • Agriculture – e.g. sowing, fertilising, harvest and picking
  • Medicine – e.g. testing and surgery
  • Domestic – e.g. robot vacuum cleaner and lawn mowers
  • Entertainment – e.g. toys, robot servers

🎯 Learning Objectives

  • Understand what robots are and describe their key characteristics.
  • Identify the electrical components (sensors, microprocessors, actuators) in robots.
  • Understand the roles that robots perform in industry, agriculture, medicine, transport, domestic and entertainment.
  • Analyse the advantages and disadvantages of using robots.
Why This Matters: Robotics is a branch of Computer Science that incorporates the design, construction and operation of robots. Understanding the core components and the real-world trade-offs of automation is essential for the IGCSE exam.

Task 1: Characteristics of Robots ★★★ Mild

In Progress

Read the information below and answer the questions. You must answer all 3 before moving on.

The Three Characteristics

  • ✔ They have a mechanical structure or framework
  • ✔ They have electrical components such as sensors, microprocessors and actuators
  • ✔ They are programmable

Electrical Components

  • Sensors – detect changes in the environment (e.g. cameras, microphones, tactile sensors)
  • Microprocessors – process data and decide what to do
  • Actuators – carry out actions (e.g. motors that move arms, legs or wheels)

Task 2: Roles Robots Perform ★★★ Medium

Locked

Read the descriptions below and answer the questions. You must answer all 4 before moving on.

Industry

Robots are used in car manufacture and packing products. They are more accurate, can work without breaks, and can work in hazardous environments.

Agriculture

Robots sow seeds, apply fertiliser, harvest crops, and use GPS for navigation. Drones survey fields.

Medicine

Robotic arms make incisions, remove tissue, and stitch patients. They are more accurate and don't have tremors like humans.

Domestic & Entertainment

Domestic robots vacuum and mow lawns. Entertainment robots (like Aibo) mimic pets and interact using sensors and actuators.

Task 3: Advantages & Disadvantages ★★★ Medium

Locked

Read the information below and answer the questions. You must answer all 3 before moving on.

Advantages and disadvantages will be different for every robot, but common advantages and disadvantages are listed below.

Common Advantages

  • ✔ Reduces the cost / need for humans to do the task
  • ✔ Works faster
  • ✔ Is more accurate / efficient
  • ✔ Is able to work in dangerous environments / reduces the risks to humans
  • ✔ Is able to work 24 x 7 / works without a break

Common Disadvantages

  • ✘ The cost of purchasing the robot (or replacing it if it breaks)
  • ✘ The robot being unreliable / less reliable than a human
  • ✘ The robot not doing as good a job as a human
  • ✘ Jobs being lost as a result of the robot
  • ✘ A loss of contact with real people

💡 Hint: Think about cost, safety, jobs, and the environment.
Example advantages: No driver required, so reduced costs; The robot can work 24 hours a day; The robot can work without breaks; The robot may be programmed to drive in the most efficient manner, reducing the environmental impact.

Example disadvantages: Someone may lose their job or there may be fewer jobs available; There is a high initial cost in the research/development/purchase of the vehicles; If there is an error in the safety systems/programming, the machine could harm people.

Lesson Overview

This lesson introduces students to robotics, covering what robots are, their characteristics, the roles they perform, and their advantages and disadvantages. Students will complete Tasks 1–4, the Schoolbox worksheet, and an extension research task.

Timing

  • 0–10 min — Starter & Objectives
  • 10–25 min — Task 1 (Characteristics of Robots)
  • 25–40 min — Task 2 (Roles of Robots) & Industrial Robot Video
  • 40–55 min — Task 3 (Advantages & Disadvantages) & Agriculture Video
  • 55–65 min — Main Task (Schoolbox Worksheet)
  • 65–80 min — Task 4 (Robot Bus Analysis) & Transport Video
  • 80–95 min — Extension Task (Domestic Robot Research)
  • 95–100 min — Review and Pack Up

Key Teaching Points

  • Use the NAO robot to illustrate sensors, microprocessors, and actuators.
  • Emphasise the real-world trade-offs of automation in different industries.
  • Ensure students understand the difference between advantages and disadvantages, and can give specific examples.
  • Use the videos to bring the content to life and spark discussion.
